# UCLA

The podcast's most-visited university, and the only one that appears as both a research institution and a personal biography: [Judea Pearl](judea-pearl.md) has been on the CS faculty since the department was formed around 1969-70 and did both of his revolutions there — Bayesian networks and the causal calculus ([episode](../sources/turing-award-winner-early-ai-llm.md)) — while host Ryan Peterman was an undergraduate there, and [Carey Nachenberg](carey-nachenberg.md) — GoogleX Chief Scientist and Symantec Fellow — has lectured CS there for 25 years alongside his industry career; Ryan was his student ([GoogleX chief scientist](../sources/googlex-chief-scientist-on-imposter.md)).

Pearl's 2026-07-27 interview is the research half. He was hired to teach computer memory hardware, drifted through pattern recognition and image compression into game-playing AI (where he proved alpha-beta pruning optimal, surprising Donald Knuth), and then spent decades on reasoning under uncertainty and [causality](../concepts/causality.md) — while describing academia itself as "the most dogmatic, conservative, anti-progress [community] that we have invented" and telling his own students to rebel against him ([episode](../sources/turing-award-winner-early-ai-llm.md), [academia-vs-industry](../concepts/academia-vs-industry.md)).

Two further episodes anchor the teaching side. Nachenberg's interview treats UCLA as his teaching laboratory, including an [AI-era](../concepts/ai-era-engineering.md) policy experiment he regrets: letting students use LLMs on projects made project scores diverge sharply from exam understanding, so he's banning them for projects while allowing them for learning ([episode](../sources/googlex-chief-scientist-on-imposter.md)). And the "Industry Secrets" episode is a recorded Q&A talk Ryan gave at UCLA with Ricky (Staff at [Google](google.md) by 28) for UPE (Upsilon Pi Epsilon), the club both belonged to as students — over 250 RSVPs for a rapid-fire distillation of what two Staff engineers wish they'd known before graduating: luck surface area, visibility, [Big Tech](../concepts/big-tech-culture.md) before [startups](../concepts/startups-and-founding.md), and why perfect code isn't enough ([UCLA talk](../sources/industry-secrets-we-wish-we-knew.md)).
